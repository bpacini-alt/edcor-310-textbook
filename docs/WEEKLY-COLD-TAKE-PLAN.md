# The Skeptical Educator: Weekly Cold Take Plan

**Status:** Working plan; candidate topics and the first run are still to be selected.  
**Owner and on-camera host:** Benjamin Pacini  
**Format:** Weekly video episodes published to YouTube under *The Skeptical Educator*, with a possible student-reading companion when an episode fits the course.

## Purpose

Build a repeatable series in which Benjamin takes a timely or widely repeated claim about education, examines what the evidence actually supports, and offers a clear, calibrated conclusion. “Cold Take” should make the series accessible and opinionated without letting speed outrun accuracy.

The episodes are public work first. They may also become optional Joy of Learning resources when the topic, evidence, and timing fit a particular course week. Student use is a deliberate second decision, not an automatic consequence of publishing an episode.

## Working principles

- Identify the claim precisely. Separate what is being claimed from what the evidence can establish.
- Give the strongest fair version of the position before evaluating it.
- Prefer primary research, systematic reviews, and authoritative data where available. Check that each source supports the specific statement attached to it.
- Distinguish findings, interpretation, uncertainty, and value judgments. Say when evidence is mixed, limited, indirect, or not yet available.
- Make the conclusion as strong as the evidence allows, and no stronger. A “cold take” may be decisive; it should not be careless.
- Keep the episode focused on one central question. Save worthwhile side questions for another candidate.
- Correct errors openly if later evidence changes the conclusion.

## Episode workflow

1. **Collect candidates.** Benjamin submits several possible topics. Capture the source or example that prompted each idea, without treating a viral claim as established fact.
2. **Triage the batch.** For each candidate, assess clarity, audience interest, evidence quality, freshness, distinctiveness, and likely student relevance. Note where a topic needs more research or is not yet ready.
3. **Choose and order a first run.** Select a manageable set from the batch, then order it for variety and a coherent publishing rhythm. Keep the weekly cadence; decide the batch size after seeing the candidates.
4. **Build a research note.** Record the exact claim, the best evidence for and against it, what remains uncertain, and source links with short notes on what each source supports.
5. **Draft the episode plan.** Use the outline below. The goal is a clear argument Benjamin can deliver naturally on camera, not a polished script unless he wants one.
6. **Review for accuracy and fairness.** Check claims against sources, identify overstatement or missing context, and mark any line that should be updated before recording.
7. **Record and publish.** Benjamin records the video and publishes it to YouTube. Prepare a title, description, source list, and captions/transcript; review auto-captions for names and technical terms when available.
8. **Decide on student use.** If the episode supports a specific week, prepare a short reading-menu blurb with a realistic time estimate and a clear stopping point if the full video is longer than the assigned portion. Add it to the Joy of Learning menu only after the episode is published and the fit is clear.
9. **Update the tracker.** Record the YouTube URL, publication date, transcript/caption status, student-reading decision, and any follow-up or correction.

## Episode outline

Adapt this to the topic; it is a guide, not a required formula.

1. **Hook:** What claim or moment are we examining?
2. **The cold take:** What is the concise answer?
3. **What the claim gets right:** What is its strongest defensible point?
4. **What the evidence shows:** What are the most relevant findings, and how strong are they?
5. **What needs qualification:** What context, limitations, or competing explanations matter?
6. **What educators should take away:** What can a teacher reasonably do or conclude?
7. **Close:** Restate the calibrated conclusion and point to sources or a useful next question.

## Student-reading decision

For each published episode, make a separate **include / do not include / revisit** decision. Include it when it:

- connects clearly to a learning goal or assigned chapter for that week;
- gives students a useful example of evaluating an educational claim with evidence;
- is understandable without requiring the whole podcast series;
- can be assigned with a reasonable time estimate and accessible captions or transcript.

When a segment is more suitable than the full episode, identify the exact start and stop point and label the assigned duration as a recommended stopping point. Do not describe a clip as the source's full length. Keep the student-facing blurb inviting and neutral about conclusions: students should know what question they will examine, not be told what they must think.
