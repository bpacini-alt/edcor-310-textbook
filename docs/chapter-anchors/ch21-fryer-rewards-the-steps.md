# Fryer: Reward the Steps

- **Book / chapter:** *Joy of Learning*, Chapter 21, “Executive Function & ADHD.”
- **Anchor:** Roland Fryer’s input-based incentives.
- **Core idea:** Rewards can guide learning when they reinforce concrete actions students can control, making the next step clearer than a distant test-score goal.
- **Chapter source:** [`etb-live/chapter21.html`](../../etb-live/chapter21.html), main `772e43195579a2734f1744fbc443e4bec8e39daa`; Research section, “Reward the steps, not the score.”
- **Scene mapping:** Fryer stands beside a chalkboard contrasting grades as outcomes with a checklist of learning steps. The students by the inputs checklist hold reward envelopes. The student by grades holds an assessment paper.
- **Misconception to avoid:** Do not imply Fryer tested only input-based rewards or that they worked for every student. His New York and Chicago trials paid for outcomes; Dallas paid for books read and quizzes passed, with gains for some students.
- **Placement:** Immediately after the title card and before the opening hook. Since the November 26, 2026 reorganization (PR #182), the hook itself explains the Fryer trials, so the caption is a short label (“Reward the steps”) instead of the trial summary. The separate “Reward the steps, not the score” research box was removed; the Daily Report Card card points back to the hook.
