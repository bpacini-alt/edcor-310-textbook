# Chapter 16: Learned helplessness and student self-talk

- **Book:** Joy of Learning
- **Chapter:** 16, “Agency and Self-Belief”
- **Anchor:** Passivity can be the default after uncontrollable adversity; experience teaches control.
- **Selected figures:** The three-dog shuttle-box comparison and the math-drill student’s “I am not a math person” self-talk.
- **Placement:** Put the dog image after the Seligman–Maier research and the 2016 reinterpretation in “Learned Helplessness.” Put the student image after the two-column attribution table in “Attribution,” alongside the example of a story that can shut a student down.
- **Scene mapping:** The dogs make the historical comparison visible: separate animals with different prior histories respond differently when an escape route is available. The student scene translates the broader idea into a classroom: a difficult task can become a fixed story about ability.
- **Guardrails:** The dog responses reflect different histories, not a single identical experience. The chapter notes that much of the newer neural evidence comes from rats and has not established the same mechanism in people. The student image is an illustrative classroom application, not a diagnosis of learned helplessness.
