# Chapter 22: Selective Attention and the Gorilla Study

- **Book:** Joy of Learning (EDCOR 310)
- **Chapter:** 22, “Attention, Memory & Cognitive Load”
- **Anchor:** Count the passes; notice what attention filters out
- **Core idea:** Focused attention can help a learner stay with a task while also causing them to miss an unexpected event outside the focus of attention.
- **Scene mapping:** The opening illustration shows a participant tracking a basketball video while a researcher observes. The later companion keeps the same scene and adds a person in a gorilla costume to the television footage. This visual sequence sets up Simons and Chabris's inattentional-blindness finding.
- **Misconception to avoid:** A viewer who misses the gorilla was not necessarily inattentive; the assigned counting task directed attention toward the passes.
- **Chapter source:** `etb-live/chapter22.html`, “Major concept 2 · When Attention Is Too Busy to Wander,” paragraph on perceptual load and the Simons and Chabris (1999) experiment.
