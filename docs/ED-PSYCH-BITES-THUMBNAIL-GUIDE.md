# Ed Psych Bites Thumbnail Guide

This guide records the visual approach for the short Ed Psych Bites videos.

## Thumbnail style

- Use a 16:9 landscape canvas and design for phone-size viewing.
- Lead with BYU–Idaho Brand Blue (#006EB6), white, and restrained blue accents.
- Keep a small, consistent **ED PSYCH BITES** label.
- Use one short, large, high-contrast hook. Let it add to the video title instead of repeating it word for word.
- Use one simple visual cue only when it helps explain the video's idea. Leave it out when it adds clutter.
- Keep the overall look clean, professional, and approachable. Avoid clickbait, excessive text emphasis, and unnecessary decorative effects.

## Portrait and pose

- Use a clear user-supplied photo as the likeness reference. AI-generated faces can drift; compare the result with the reference and revise if it looks like a lookalike.
- Use a still from the specific video to match the clothing and pose. Do not let a previous generated thumbnail determine the user's face.
- Choose an expression that fits the message. For affirming content about teens, use a warm, confident, direct expression rather than a skeptical or uncertain look.
- Use a gesture only when it connects directly to the video's content, such as raising a hand for a video about students raising their hands. Otherwise, keep hands out of frame to avoid awkward poses.

## Copy and layout

- Prefer a clear hook over a long sentence. Use a simple hierarchy when a longer phrase carries important nuance.
- A short bulleted list works well when the video names several ideas. For example: **TEENS NEED:** autonomy, structure, and warm relationships.
- Check spelling, facial likeness, expression, clothing, and small-screen legibility before treating a thumbnail as final.

## Examples from the pilot

- Children arguing: **ARGUING = THINKING?**
- Math-drill timers: **DO TIMERS HELP?** with a raised-hand cue.
- Adolescence: **ADOLESCENCE CAN BE HARD, BUT TEENS ARE GREAT.**
- Teens' needs: **TEENS NEED:** autonomy, structure, and warm relationships.

## Closing end screen — proposed, not settled

A brief reusable end screen may help interested viewers continue to a related video or playlist. For this channel, the current suggestion is a simple blue end screen with one relevant next video or playlist, while the final spoken sentence finishes. Avoid a long animated outro or a generic call to action. This is still a proposal, not an adopted channel rule.
