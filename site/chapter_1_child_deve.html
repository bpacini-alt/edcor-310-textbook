Unit 2 · Developmental Psychology

Unit Outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

Unit 1 gave you a way to weigh evidence and catch your own blind spots. Now point those tools at a moving target: the developing child. This unit traces the arc of a student's thinking from constructivism through social learning, follows adolescent moral reasoning as it takes a shape adults often misread, and shows how a student's sense of identity steers their behavior before you ever say a word.

Understanding how students develop is what makes the next question answerable: once you know where a student is developmentally, what actually moves them to engage? That's where Unit 3 goes next.

  • 1
    Apply principles of child and adolescent cognitive development to the design of an age-appropriate learning experience.CLO 3
  • 2
    Apply research on moral development to explain how adolescents reason about right and wrong, and what that implies for classroom practice.CLO 3
  • 3
    Evaluate competing claims about the relative influence of parenting versus genetics and peers, using the standards of evidence from Unit 1.CLO 1CLO 3
  • 4
    Apply principles of identity development to explain how a student's sense of self shapes their classroom engagement and behavior.CLO 3
